Scenario 01

"Engineer says 3 months. CEO wants it in 4 weeks. What do you do?"

I run a scope triage immediately — what's the 20% that delivers 80% of value? I work with the engineer to define a shippable MVP that meets the CEO's intent, not just their deadline. Then I align both sides on the new definition of done. Time pressure is a prioritization tool, not an enemy.

Scenario 02

"CEO wants a feature. Users don't. What do you do?"

I bring data, not opinions. User research, session recordings, conversion metrics — I make the user's voice undeniable. Then I find the underlying business goal the CEO is trying to achieve and propose an alternative that serves both. The CEO is solving a problem too — I just need to understand which one.

Scenario 03

"User says X. Data says Y. Which do you trust?"

Neither alone. Users tell you what they feel; data tells you what they do. The truth is usually in the gap between them. I interview to understand the "why" behind the data anomaly — then I design around the root cause, not the symptom. At Savax, users said onboarding was "fine." Data said 30% dropped off. We fixed the real problem.
